Token-Level Backtracking

for Corrective Reasoning in Large Language Models

Siddharth Mohanty University of California, Santa Cruz

Paper (PDF) Code Interactive Trace Results Citation

Autoregressive language models generate text irrevocably: each token conditions on every token before it. Once a mistaken inference enters the context, the downstream reasoning chain becomes polluted and the model is forced to rationalize a false premise. We propose Token-Level Backtracking, an inference-time mechanism that detects reasoning errors via unsupervised signals, uses a lightweight draft model to check whether a substantively different continuation exists, and rewinds the KV cache to the earlier “weak node” where the error began.

On GSM8K, Llama‑3.1‑70B improves from 74.2% to 82.1% accuracy while retaining 88% of baseline throughput—without retraining, fine‑tuning, or supervised verifiers.

1. Motivation

Existing inference-time strategies such as beam search, self-consistency, and multi-pass reflection address errors retrospectively by sampling entire replacement trajectories or critiquing finished outputs. While reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1-Zero naturally learn under reinforcement learning to emit self-correction tokens (“Wait…”) mid-solution, standard decoding still leaves the erroneous tokens in context. Rather than generating more text to argue past a mistake, token-level backtracking deletes back to the error locus and resamples.

2. Methodology

We treat the token stream as a dynamic tree rather than a flat list. Most tokens are sound; a few are polluting nodes where reasoning becomes contradictory or uncertain.

Unsupervised Error Detection

Potential reasoning errors are flagged at inference time using five unsupervised signals:

Trigger tokens
Linguistic markers such as Wait, Actually, or But that means… indicating the model is about to contradict itself.
Entropy spikes
Sudden jumps in token-level perplexity when the model struggles to justify a flawed prior step.
Weak nodes
Steps where the top two token probabilities are nearly tied (p1 − p2 < ε). These indices are bookmarked as uncertainty checkpoints for later rollbacks.
Likelihood contrast
Checking whether the model, conditioned on a corrective context, confidently refuses to regenerate its earlier claim.
Self-consistency
Sampling short continuations to test whether trajectories diverge from the suspect statement.

Speculative Divergence Gate

Rolling back discards the KV cache for the abandoned path. Before committing to a rollback, we repurpose speculative decoding as a semantic probe: a small draft model generates a short lookahead from the candidate checkpoint, and we measure the cosine distance between the hidden representations of the draft branch and the current trajectory:

d = 1 − cos(hdraft, hcurrent), rewind only if d > τ(1)

If the alternative branch is merely a surface rephrasing (d ≤ τ, with τ = 0.35 in our experiments), the rollback is aborted. This gate filters out roughly 73% of candidate triggers, preserving throughput.

Dynamic Rollback & Resampling

  1. Rewind to the inception point. Because models typically recognize an error several tokens after making it, the decoder rolls back to the nearest weak-node checkpoint rather than just deleting the trigger token.
  2. Contrastive resampling. Applying a −30.0 logit penalty to the rejected token sequence forces the generator onto the alternative branch identified by the draft model.
  3. Tree-based KV retention. The rejected branch is marked inactive rather than immediately erased, allowing the decoder to revert if the new branch yields lower probability.
  4. Loop guardrails. Enforcing a limit of 3 backtracks per reasoning step and 50 per sequence prevents repetitive rollback loops.

3. Results

Across four mathematical and coding benchmarks, Token-Level Backtracking outperforms standard decoding and post-hoc correction baselines while maintaining 88% of standard autoregressive throughput. Gains are largest on AIME, where solutions require 10–15 chained steps and a single arithmetic slip invalidates the final answer.

Because the mechanism relies only on output logits and hidden-state cosine distances, it transfers across model families without modification. Evaluating across four model pairs yields consistent gains of 5.2–7.9 points on GSM8K and 4.9–6.8 points on HumanEval pass@1.

Varying the draft model size for a fixed Llama-3.1-70B generator shows that the 8B draft captures 91% of the accuracy gain of full 70B self-drafting at 89% of baseline speed. A 1B draft is too weak to separate real semantic divergence from noise (0.61 gate precision, wasting 39% of rollbacks), whereas 70B self-drafting nearly halves throughput.

4. Ablations

Removing the speculative divergence gate causes the decoder to rewind nearly 3× as often on surface-level rephrasings, reducing accuracy by 2.8 points and increasing latency by 31%. Removing the contrastive logit penalty drops accuracy by 2.1 points as the generator occasionally re-derives the same erroneous step. Removing weak-node checkpoints forces rollbacks to use a fixed token window that frequently overshoots or undershoots the error locus (−3.3 points).

5. Error Analysis

We manually categorized 200 GSM8K examples where backtracking changed the final answer relative to the baseline. Of these, 156 (78.0%) flipped an incorrect answer to correct, 31 (15.5%) reached the same correct answer via an alternative derivation, and 13 (6.5%) introduced a new error.

Among the 156 successful corrections, arithmetic slips accounted for 41%, variable misattribution for 23%, logical inversions for 18%, premature conclusions for 12%, and unit conversion errors for 6%. The 13 regressions primarily occurred when the baseline arrived at the right answer through two cancelling errors; backtracking fixed the first error and exposed the second.
